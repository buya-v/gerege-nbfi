############ 1. THE MECHANISM, DEMONSTRATED ############
engine under test: grep (BSD grep, GNU compatible) 2.6.0-FreeBSD
shim in an interactive shell: /usr/bin/grep -> a ugrep shell function (GNU-compatible)
shim in `bash script.sh`   : NOT inherited; scripts get /usr/bin/grep

  BRE  '\.sh$\|\.py$'                -> 1   (2 lines should match)
    ...its exit status               ->    exit=0  <-- undercounts AND reports SUCCESS
  BRE  '\.sh\|\.py'                  -> 2   (alternation ALONE is fine: not a dropped branch)
  BRE  '\.py$\|\.sh$'                -> 1   (order swapped: the OTHER branch survives)
  BRE  vs line 'a.sh$X'              -> 1   (mid-pattern \$ matched a LITERAL dollar: the proof)
  ERE  -E '\.sh$|\.py$'              -> 2   (ERE: correct)

  CONCLUSION: in BSD BRE, `$` is an anchor only at the END of the pattern and
  `^` only at the START. Any earlier occurrence is a literal character. The
  first branch of an anchored BRE alternation is therefore DEAD, silently, at exit 0.

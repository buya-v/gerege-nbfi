***** THE THREE DATE CELLS, AND WHERE EACH ONE COMES FROM. STATE THE ZONE BESIDE EVERY DATE. ***** CLAUDE.md: two time zones, no DST, NEVER hard-code an offset.   request.latest_closing_date 2026-08-26 -- READ FROM THE TARGET, twice, and the builder refuses if the two disagree: GET /glclosures returned closingDate "2026-08-26" [B1-glclosures-list.json] and the acc_gl_closure readback row is `1 | 1 | 2026-08-26` [B1-closure-state.txt].
  request.business_date 2026-08-28 -- MEASURED BY A TWO-SIDED BRACKET, NOT READ. /businessdates returned 404 on that instance [S-07-businessdates.status], so no endpoint could be asked. Instead: an entry dated 2026-08-28 was ACCEPTED (HTTP 200) and an entry dated 2026-08-29 was REFUSED with error.msg.glJournalEntry.invalid.future.date, seconds apart on the same instance. Accepted at D means businessDate >= D; refused at D+1 means businessDate < D+1; therefore businessDate == D == 2026-08-28. The builder RECOMPUTES that bracket from the committed bytes -- it checks both statuses, checks the refusing arm's globalisation code is the FUTURE-DATE one and not some other 403, and checks the two dates are exactly one calendar day apart -- and refuses to emit if any of it fails. THIS IS STRICTLY BETTER PROVENANCE THAN LDG-REFUSE-04 AND LDG-REFUSE-05 HAVE, whose business_date cells T329 named as already ambiguous: those were DERIVED, this one is BRACKETED.

***** THE ZONE OBSERVATION, WHICH IS EVIDENCE AND NOT A COINCIDENCE. ***** The run landed at 2026-08-27T17:33Z -- the one hour of that day where UTC read 2026-08-27 and Asia/Ulaanbaatar read 2026-08-28. An instance reading the business date in UTC would have REFUSED the sibling arm dated 2026-08-28 as future. It ACCEPTED. So the guard reads the TENANT zone, observed rather than assumed. It is also a warning, and the reason the paragraph above states the zone beside every date: a reader who assumes UTC would mis-order these three dates by one day and conclude the opposite of what was measured.

***** createdDate AND submittedOnDate ARE NOT GRADED BY THIS VECTOR, DELIBERATELY, AND THE ARGUMENT IS T329's. ***** The REST readback taken beside this capture carries `"createdDate": [2026,8,28]` and `"submittedOnDate": [2026,8,28]` on all three entries [B1-ACCEPT-06-readback-rest.json] while the entries' transactionDate is [2026,8,27]. TWO DIFFERENT QUANTITIES SHARING ONE NAME, and this capture SHOWS BOTH AT ONCE: at the same instant, /glclosures reported createdDate "2026-08-27" (the UTC/JVM day) and /journalentries reported [2026,8,28] (the tenant business day) [B1-glclosures-list.json vs B1-ACCEPT-06-readback-rest.json]. GRADING EITHER AS A LITERAL WOULD BAKE AN UP-TO-EIGHT-HOUR WINDOW INTO A PARITY VECTOR: the value is the wall-clock day at insertion, so re-running the committed recipe on a different day -- or the SAME day either side of 16:00Z -- produces a different number and the vector would pass or fail on what time of day CI ran. The exclusion is by construction and not by omission: this vector is built from the DATABASE readback (acc_gl_journal_entry joined to acc_gl_account), which has no created-date column at all, and the vector schema has no field that could hold one. The REST bytes are committed beside the capture and are graded by NOTHING. Note the field that IS gradeable and IS graded: the DB's `entry_date`, which is the caller's transaction date, is a business fact and not a clock reading -- and it is asserted by the builder rather than merely copied.

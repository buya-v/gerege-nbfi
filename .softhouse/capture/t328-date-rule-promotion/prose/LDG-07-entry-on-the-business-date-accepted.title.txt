THE ORACLE ACCEPTS A MANUAL JOURNAL ENTRY DATED EXACTLY ON THE BUSINESS DATE, WHICH IS THE WIRE PROOF THAT :630's COMPARISON IS STRICT. T327's B-2 arm POSTed a balanced three-leg body dated 2026-08-28 to a tenant with NO GLClosure at all, and the oracle returned HTTP 200 and wrote three journal entries; the same shape dated 2026-08-29 -- one day later -- drew HTTP 403 error.msg.glJournalEntry.invalid.future.date with args[0].value echoing its own 2026-08-29. THOSE TWO ARMS TOGETHER DO NOT MERELY CONFIRM STRICTNESS, THEY MEASURE THE BUSINESS DATE ON THE WIRE: accepted at 2026-08-28 means businessDate >= 2026-08-28, refused at 2026-08-29 means businessDate < 2026-08-29, so businessDate == 2026-08-28, with no clock read by this store and no endpoint consulted -- /businessdates returned 404 on that instance. Until this vector the store pinned only the REFUSING side of :629-631, so a port implementing that comparison NON-STRICTLY -- !isBefore(transactionDate, businessDate) where the oracle has isAfter -- passed LDG-REFUSE-05 while refusing every entry booked on the current business day, which is most of them. This vector is the only thing in the corpus that can tell > from >=.

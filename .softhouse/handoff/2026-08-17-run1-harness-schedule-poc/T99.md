### F-2 (T85 P3) — a PATH-poisoned `shasum` defeated the digest pin and left no diff trace

`preconditions.sh:195` computed the pin's first operand with the bare word `shasum`, so a `shasum`
earlier on `$PATH` printing the pinned constant made **both operands the attacker's** — worse than
the tautology it replaced, because the recipe now *looks* hardened and nothing in any diff moves.

**What landed.** A new `t36/sha256.sh`, sourced by `preconditions.sh`, `recapture.sh`,
`emiloop-probe.sh` and `recreate-products.sh`. Three properties: (1) **absolute paths only**, in
root-owned system directories, for the digest tools *and* the `awk`/`env`/`mktemp`/`rm` that drive
them — `/usr/local/bin` and `/opt/homebrew/bin` are deliberately not candidates because they are
user-writable; each tool runs under `/usr/bin/env -i` with a fixed minimal environment (so
`PERL5LIB`/`PERL5OPT`/`PYTHONPATH`/`OPENSSL_CONF` cannot inject either), python3 additionally with
`-I`. (2) **A known-answer test per tool, every run** — sha256("") and sha256("abc") before a tool is
allowed to answer anything. (3) **Two independent implementations must agree** — perl `shasum`,
C `sha256sum`, LibreSSL `openssl dgst`, CPython `hashlib`; fewer than two survivors, or any
disagreement, and the caller gets a refusal and **no digest at all**. In `preconditions.sh` an
untrustworthy instrument is now a **BREACH** (`bad`), never a silent pass.

**The brief asked specifically whether the cross-check compares or merely prints. It compares**
(`sha256.sh:158`): `if [ "$_d" != "$_first" ]` → sets `SHA256_ERROR` and `return 1`. And the
resolution itself cannot be poisoned, because there is no resolution step to poison: `_sha256_path`
is a literal table of absolute paths, and extending it means editing a committed file, which shows
in a diff. **This is exercised, not asserted**, by leg 2d below, where a liar that *passes* the KAT
is still caught.

### BLAST RADIUS — settled from committed evidence, with no re-capture (T135 §8, folded in by T152)

*How many committed Path B captures were taken through a `preconditions.sh` that could pass
vacuously, and does anything follow for their admissibility?*

the repository, which is why no re-capture was needed. **The discriminator, stated so a later reader
can re-run the reasoning rather than trust the conclusion:** each of the three vacuous assertions

positive PASS is impossible over an empty stream.

## Pattern candidates

* **Ask of every check: what is the observation, and could it be produced by the check never
  running?** Applied mechanically to T99's own work it found four more instances — three vacuous
  prohibition PASSes, two verdict predicates that read an absence as a positive, an exit code printed
  and discarded, and a report that dropped one of the three lines it existed to show. Two of them
  were introduced *in the task sent to fix the previous one*, which is now the third fire running.
* **A count of zero and an empty string are two facts wearing one face.** Any `grep -c … = 0`,
  `[ -z "$x" ]` or `wc -l = 0` that adjudicates a **prohibition** needs a liveness operand — evidence
  that the input existed. Without it, "clean" and "never looked" are the same output.
* **A sweep for a defect shape is only as good as its pattern.** T99's grep for the vacuous-check
  shape was `grep -c\|grep -ac\|wc -l` and therefore could not see `grep -icE`, which is how the two
  worst instances in the tree were written. When sweeping for a shape, **enumerate the spellings, or
  match the flag letters as a class** — and say in the report what the pattern cannot match.
* **A proof must be re-run from the artefact the proof exports.** T99b's first fix to
  `preconditions.sh` changed nothing in the f2/f3 transcripts, because the proofs export
  `git archive HEAD` and the fix was uncommitted. A red/green harness that reads from git needs the
  fix committed *before* the transcript means anything — and the silent no-change is exactly what a
  no-op fix looks like.
* **Regenerate a killed worker's committed transcripts before believing them, and diff.** Ten out of
  ten reproduced byte-identically here, which is what made it safe to spend the remaining budget
  attacking the rig instead of rebuilding it.

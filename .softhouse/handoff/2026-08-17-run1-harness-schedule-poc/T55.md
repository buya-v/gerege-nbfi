## Findings

### T55-N1 (P2) — **T48-N4's stated promotion condition is too strong, and would reject a shape that does grade the arm**

T48-N4, as recorded in `RESUME.md`, `gates.md` and DEC-1's `DayCountActualActual` note, requires an
admitted vector to *"cross a leap boundary with a **non-zero first segment**"*. `LB-DEC31` crosses a
leap boundary with a **zero** first segment and still grades the arm, by **6 015 minor units**:
segments `0/366 + 31/365` give `22014.25`, while a no-arm port keeping the from-date year's actual
366 returns `21954.10`.

The correct necessary condition, re-derived: the arm coincides with the plain branch exactly when
**every year the period touches has the same length as the year the plain branch would divide by** —
which is the from-date year. So the condition is *"the period spans at least two calendar years of
**differing length**"*, and the first segment's size is irrelevant. Note this is a **correction to a
condition recorded in a ratified DEC-n's commentary**, so acting on it is a `user`/driver call, not
mine — I record it and change nothing. The practical guidance is unaffected in the direction that
matters: the shapes T48-N4 tells you to reject (all-365 runs) are still exactly the ones
`LB-NONLEAP`/`LB-DEC15NL` observe as blind.

### T55-N2 (P2) — a pair reporting 0 cells does **not** mean the capture fails to grade the arm

`LB-F29CROSS` and `LB-MULTI3F` report **0 of 43 cells** on every available setting pair, yet **all
six of their captures grade both naive ports** — by 17 850 and 71 014 minor units respectively, the
second being the largest clean grader in the set. The reason is structural: `daysInYearCustomStrategy`
only decides **whether** the arm is entered; it never changes the arm's denominators, which come from
`Year.of(actualYear).length()` directly. When both legs enter the arm there is nothing left for the
setting to move, but the arm's *value* still differs from every no-arm reading. **A vector-selection
rule that promoted only shapes with non-zero pair counts would discard the two best graders here.**

### T55-N3 (P3) — a pure single-effect isolation is **impossible** in the 366 → 365 direction

Effect (a) (`getNumberOfDays`' 366 → 365 substitution) fires only when the **interest period's
from-date year is leap** [VERIFIED: `:1348-1351`]. In the leap-out direction the crossing period's
from-date is necessarily inside the leap year, so effect (a) always co-fires with effect (b). Observed
on `LB-LEAPOUT`: period 1 (non-crossing, 2024-11-01 → 2024-12-01) separates by effect (a) alone
(`21245.90` with 366 vs `21304.11` with 365 — 5 821 minor units), and period 2 by effect (b). The
365 → 366 direction *does* admit a pure isolation, because the from-date year is non-leap — which is
what `LB-LEAPIN` and `T48B-PUREB` are. Recorded so no later task spends a fire hunting a leap-out
pure isolation that cannot exist.

### T55-N4 (P3) — `f_arm` can be **exactly** an integer, and `LB-MULTI3F` is that case

`213/365 + 366/366 + 152/365 = 2` exactly, because 213 + 152 = 365. The observed interest is therefore
`1200000 × 0.216 × 2 = 518400.00` to the cent, with no rounding residue anywhere. A useful
calibration row: any port that produces anything other than `518400.00` here has an error large
enough to survive an exact-integer factor.

### T55-N5 (P3) — the EMI solve couples periods, so "both legs took the same branch" ≠ "the rows match"

`T48B-QTR`'s period 1 and 2 both take the arm, yet its p7/p4 period-2 interests differ
(`227229.96` vs `227225.29`) because period 3 separates through effect (a), which moves the EMI, which
moves period 1's principal split, which moves period 2's opening balance. This is why T55's isolation
shapes (`LB-F29CROSS`, `LB-MULTI3`, `LB-MULTI3F`, `LB-DEC15*`, `LB-DEC31`) are **single-period**. A
reviewer comparing multi-period schedules cannot attribute a per-period difference to a per-period
cause.

## Confounds ruled out

* **The driver's D-2 warning (final-period date adjustment on Path B).** Every T55 comparison is
  **same-path, same-request-shape, one setting changed**, so a date adjustment would apply identically
  to both legs and cancel. I made **no cross-path comparison**. I also checked the observed due dates
  directly: no T55 capture's dates differ between its p7, p3 and p4 legs — `row*.dueDate` and
  `row*.fromDate` are among the compared cells and appear in **zero** of the differing-cell lists, on
  all 33 captures. Invariant I4 (due dates strictly monotonic) passes on all 33. So no date moved,
  and no observed difference in this pass can be a disguised date effect.
* **Charges and holidays.** No T55 capture carries a charge or a holiday, so neither the
  `loanCharges`-null limit nor the `holidayDetailDTO`-null limit at
  `ProgressiveLoanScheduleGenerator.java:83` is reached, and the T46 N46-1 ambient charge-rounding
  leak is not reached either.
* **`interestRecognitionOnDisbursementDate`.** It selects the arm's segment boundary (31 Dec vs
  1 Jan) [VERIFIED: `:1578-1584`], so it is a genuine confound for this arm. It is `f` on all three
  products, asserted out of PostgreSQL before every capture, and identical across the three — so it
  is held constant. T51 separately observed the product/request flag to be inert on this path; T55
  neither relies on nor re-litigates that.
* **`SAME_AS_REPAYMENT_PERIOD`.** Would return at `:1516-1524` **before** the partial-period branch
  and make the arm unreachable. All three products are `interest_calculated_in_period_enum = 0`
  (DAILY), asserted before capture.

## Money-math notes

* **No float in any stored or compared value.** Raw responses are float-shaped on the wire (finding
  **T44-X1**) and are kept unmodified as the canonical artefact; every one has an exact-text sidecar
  built with `json.loads(text, parse_float=str, parse_int=str)`, so no binary double is ever
  constructed. All 33 sidecars carry **zero** bare JSON numbers, asserted in code. Negative leg
  **N7** removes the `parse_float=str` and shows the guard catching it.
* **All comparison and re-derivation run on `decimal.Decimal` built from exact decimal text**, at
  precision 19 / `ROUND_HALF_UP`, mirroring `BigDecimal(mc)` and the
  `setScale(mc.getPrecision(), mc.getRoundingMode())` at `:1979`.
* **Differences are reported in integer minor units** throughout. MNT = ISO 4217 496, minor unit 2;
  invariant I6 confirms every observed money cell is at most 2 dp on all 33 captures.
* **The only non-integer input is the rate** `21.6` — a rate, not money. Written as exact decimal
  text in the request; Fineract parses request numbers to `BigDecimal`; the re-derivation uses
  `Decimal("21.6") / Decimal(100)`. Comparison is **exact string/`Decimal` equality, never a
  tolerance** — finding **T17-F6**: a transcribed 12-dp factor hides divergence in digits 13+. No
  factor in this pass is transcribed at reduced precision; the arm's factors are recomputed, not
  copied.
* **The arm's rounding shape, for the porter:** each segment is divided **separately** at precision
  19 and the quotients are **added** at precision 19 [`:1563`], then multiplied by the rate and
  `setScale(19, HALF_UP)` [`:1976-1979`]. A port that sums the day counts first and divides once
  would agree on all-365 shapes and can diverge elsewhere. `LB-MULTI3`'s three-segment factor is the
  shape most likely to expose that, and its observed value is `517986.72`.

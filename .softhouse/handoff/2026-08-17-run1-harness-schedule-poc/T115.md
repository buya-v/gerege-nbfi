### What this sweep could NOT have found (P-26)

A sweep whose limits are unstated reads as exhaustive. Mine is not.

* **It is a sweep of the T91 rig only** — `.softhouse/capture/t91/` and the two precondition shims.
  It says nothing about `pathb/t36/preconditions.sh` (the rig itself, read-only to me), `attest.py`,
  `recapture.sh`, `t51-negative.sh`, `t55-negative-tests.sh`, or any sibling worker's capture tree.
  Those contain checks of exactly this shape and nobody has swept them.
* **A vacuous pass that needs the oracle to be WRONG, not absent, is out of reach.** I probed a
  *dead*-oracle transcript shape (13 transcripts all `EXIT=1`, no certification sentence) and
  `verdict.sh` exits 1 with three `REGRESSION` rows — **but only because `A4c`, `A7` and `A8` are
  the three `CLEAN` rows.** That is the *only* thing making the scorer fail against a dead oracle,
  it is accidental, and nothing said so before this paragraph. **Retyping any one of those three
  rows to `BREACH` would silently create a scorer that reports a clean sweep against an oracle that
  never answered.** Recorded as a standing hazard, not fixed — changing the expectation table is a
  substantive act, not a micro-fix.
* **I could not test the absent-tool entry point for `ugrep`**, because there is no `ugrep` on this
  host (T107b established that). What I could do is make the branch honest about not having
  measured anything, which is what V-G does.
* **A check whose vacuity depends on a real environment I cannot create** — a Linux host, a
  different `grep`, a filesystem that fails writes intermittently rather than uniformly, an oracle
  that answers slowly enough to time out mid-transcript — is untested.
* **I swept executable checks.** A claim that is vacuous *as prose* — a header sentence that
  certifies something nothing verifies — is only caught where I happened to read it. MF-3 and MF-4
  are two such; there may be more in files I did not open.
* **The census greps `.sh` and `.py` under `.softhouse/capture/`.** It cannot find an invocation
  built by string concatenation, held in a variable, made from CI or a Makefile, made through a
  symlink or a copied path, or made by a human at a prompt — which is how the negative controls are
  usually driven.

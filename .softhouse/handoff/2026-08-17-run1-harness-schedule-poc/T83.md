### Closed form — a HYPOTHESIS, and I say so

`a(r,n) = r/(1−(1+r)^−n)` and `r = annual/100/12` — i.e. the level EMI quantizes to `0.00` at scale 2

for any un-sampled rate, term, day-count or currency scale.

## Does `invariant_exemptions` exist

**Yes — it exists, the harness honours it, and it is INERT on this divergence.** I measured that
rather than assuming it in either direction.

**It exists and is real** [VERIFIED at source]:

- schema: `Exemption{invariant, reason}` and `Vector.InvariantExemptions`
  (`conformance/vector.go:684`, `:747`); the field is present, as `[]`, on every committed vector.
- admission validates it: an unknown invariant name or an empty reason makes the vector
  **inadmissible** (`conformance/admit.go:235-241`).
- grading honours it: `CheckInvariants` returns `EXEMPT` with the reason for a named invariant
  (`conformance/invariants.go:193-210`), and the reason is printed in the report on every run
  (`conformance/report.go:190-198`).
- `invPrincipalAmortizes`'s own doc comment invites it: *"A schedule that is legitimately not fully
  amortizing exempts this invariant by name and states why"* (`invariants.go:294-298`).

**And it does not reach this defect** [VERIFIED: `out/exemption-demo.json`, produced by
`src/run-exemption-demo.py` + `src/t83grade.go.txt`, which run the **real** `conformance.Run` with
the **real** Go port over a scratch store under `/tmp`; `.softhouse/vectors` was neither read nor
written]:

| variant | admissible | outcome | the diff | invariant statuses |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `invariant_exemptions: []` | yes | **FAIL** | `row 6 outstanding_principal_minor: expected 1 minor units, got 0 (delta -1)` | all six **HOLD** |
| exempting `principal_amortizes_to_zero` + `balance_roll_forward` | yes | **FAIL** | *the same single cell* | those two **EXEMPT**, other four HOLD |

Two things follow, and the second is the one that matters:

1. The vector option (a) would need is **well-formed and admissible** — 47 graded cells, 2 ungraded
   (the disbursement row's `installment_number` and `interest_minor`, which the oracle's own record
   type never carries).
2. **The exemption changes nothing.** Without it every invariant already **HOLDS**, because
   invariants are checked against *what the implementation returned* and the port's schedule does
   amortize. The exemption turns HOLD into EXEMPT and leaves the verdict identical. The G-8
   divergence is a **cell diff**, and `diffSchedule` decides FAIL before the invariant statuses are
   consulted (`grade.go:487-497`).

**So option (a) as recorded in G-8 is not reachable with the existing mechanism alone.** Its full
shape is: **change the port** to emit the oracle's stale balance, *and then* carry both exemptions —
because at that point the port's own output would violate `principal_amortizes_to_zero` **and**
`balance_roll_forward`. That is a port change. This task changes no Go logic, so I **prepared and did
not promote**: the two proposed vectors are committed at
`proposed-vector-no-exemption.json` and `proposed-vector-with-exemption.json` in the capture
directory, **not** in `.softhouse/vectors/`.

*Caveat, stated so nobody misreads the demo output:* the scratch single-vector store also emits two
`FATAL` lines — `monthend.reanchor` has no vector in it, and "no parity vector was graded" because
the only vector present FAILs. Both are artefacts of grading a one-file store and say nothing about
the vector. The verdict I rely on is the per-vector `FAIL` line and its single cell diff.

  `getPrevious().get().getOutstandingLoanBalance()` — the **previous** period's balance. The stale

  its memo.
- The actual pre-adjustment read is the filter at **`ProgressiveEMICalculator.java:1180`**
  (`.filter(rp -> rp.getOutstandingLoanBalance().isGreaterThanZero())`), in the **same method** as
  the raise at **`:1210`** (`repaymentPeriod.setEmi(adjustedEmi)`), and evaluated before it.
- The only other readers in that class are `:617` (a public query method, off the generate path) and
  `:1629` (reads the *previous* of the first repayment period). So `:1180` is the only candidate on
  this path.

The rest of the driver's re-derivation holds at every line I re-opened: the memo key at `:400` omits
`emi` while the body subtracts `getDuePrincipal()` at `:398`; `getDuePrincipal()` (`:345-350`,
`:293-295`) is a direct function of `getEmi()`; the sibling `getDueInterest()` memo (`:272-286`)
**does** declare `emi`, so the omission is asymmetric within one class; `Memo.get()` recomputes only
on a declared dependency's `hashCode` change (`Memo.java`), and `useReferenceCheck` is false for
these, so it is **value** hashing.

**So his reframing is CORRECT and I could not refute it.** G-8 is materially narrower than "the

  source and the Go comparison are reproducible from this review", but `CaptureF2.java` is not in the

  period's memoized balance during recalculation.** That is a *second* place a stale balance could
  propagate — into the *next* period's initial balance — and this task did not probe it. It is
  probably inert on single-disbursement origination, but that is an expectation, not a measurement.
- **`totalOutstandingAmount` is `"0"` on every capture in this program's corpus** (already recorded
  as a known-inert field). Here it happens to be *right* while the balance column is wrong, which is
  the first time that field has carried useful information. Worth remembering before anyone deletes
  it as always-zero.
- **The harness cannot express "the oracle is wrong here and we know it".** The vector classes are
  parity / contract-refusal / self-test, and `invariant_exemptions` reaches invariants only. If
  Buyan picks (c), a fourth expression — a per-cell *declared divergence* with a reason, comparable
  to `unrecorded_fields` but honest about *why* — is the smallest thing that would let the corpus
  record a known oracle defect without either lying about the capture or dropping the shape. Naming
  it here; designing it is not this task's.

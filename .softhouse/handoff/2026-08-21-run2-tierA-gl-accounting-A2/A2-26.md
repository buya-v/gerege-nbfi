## Readiness verdict

**Yes — a `ledger` parity vector is capturable today, and from exactly one pre-existing product:
product 46, plus the new product 55 this task created on the same admissible account set. Every
other A2 product is inadmissible, proven by re-sending its mapping, not inferred.**

But the corpus A2-15 would have inherited **could not have supported a useful ledger vector**, and
three of the reasons are not things A2-15 could have discovered without the oracle:

1. **Every journal entry in the corpus had exactly two legs.** 7 distinct transactions, 14 rows, all
   two-leg. "Splits sum to the whole" — a property invariant `/softhouse-uat` asserts — was graded by
   nothing. There are now **2 four-leg and 4 three-leg** transactions [VERIFIED: `A2-370`
   leg-count distribution].
2. **Every ledger amount in the corpus was a whole tugrik** (1,200,000 / 200,000 / 1,000,000 /
   50,000). A port that dropped or mis-rounded minor units was byte-indistinguishable from a correct
   one on every capture ever taken in slice A2. There are now legs at `270450.58`, `22049.42`,
   `889549.42`, `20298.82`, `90151.76`, `125000.62`, `25000.37`, `100000.25`, `33333.33`.
3. **A cell A2-15 would naturally have promoted is NOT STABLE.** `glAccountType` in a
   `/journalentries` response is a projection of the *account's current* classification, not of the
   entry. The identical row renders `ASSET` in `A2-088` (2026-08-21) and `INCOME` in `A2-320` (today),
   with **every other cell byte-identical** — 6 diff lines, all inside `glAccountType`
   [VERIFIED: `A2-088` vs `A2-320`]. **A ledger vector that grades `glAccountType` from this endpoint
   will go red on a GL-account edit that touched no entry.** This was G-10's re-derived claim; it is
   now an observation.

So: **capturable, from product 46 / 55 only, and A2-15 must treat `glAccountType` as excluded or
as a separately-sourced cell.**

## (iii) The `wait` restructure — bash semantics re-derived from scratch

I wrote my own two-script probe rather than reasoning from the handoff
(`.softhouse/reviews/T158-bash-signal-semantics.py`, transcript
`T158-evidence/05-bash-signal-semantics.txt`):

* **Foreground child, `SIGINT` to the script pid only, 1.0 s in:** `START` at t, trap
  fires at **t+10** — deferred for the whole `sleep 10`. Script exit `130`.
* **Backgrounded child + `wait "$P"`, same signal, same timing:** trap fires at **t+1**.
* **`wait` status fidelity control, no signal:** child exits 3; `rc=3` in *both* the
  foreground and the `wait` forms.

[VERIFIED: all three, same transcript] **T156's claim is correct on all three counts** —
bash defers a trapped signal until the current foreground child ends, `wait` is
interruptible, and `wait` yields the child's status, so `rc1` keeps foreground semantics.

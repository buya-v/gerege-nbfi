## 2. Arm 1 — future-dated entry refusal

Two probes against office 1, otherwise well-formed and balanced so the **date is the only defect**.
Debit GL 4 (`10201` Loan Portfolio), credit GL 2 (`10100` Fund Source) — both manual-allowed, enabled,
detail accounts.

| probe | transactionDate | status |
|---|---|---|
| `A1-01-future-far` | `2026-12-31` | **403** `error.msg.glJournalEntry.invalid.future.date` |
| `A1-02-future-boundary-plus1` | `2026-08-24` (business date + 1) | **403**, same code |

**Business date is `2026-08-23`**, and this is derived rather than read from an endpoint:
`enable-business-date` is **`f`** and `m_business_date` is **empty**, so
`BusinessDateReadPlatformServiceImpl.getBusinessDates()` seeds `BUSINESS_DATE` with
`DateUtils.getLocalDateOfTenant()` — today in `Asia/Ulaanbaatar` (+08). `now()` in the DB read
`2026-08-23 00:09:58+00` = 08:09 on 2026-08-23 in Ulaanbaatar. `GET /businessdate` returns `[]` and
`GET /businessdate/BUSINESS_DATE` returns 404, both consistent with the feature being off.

**Money:** every wire amount is the integer token `1000000` — no decimal point, byte-preserved under a
binary-double round trip. Fineract's `amount` is a `BigDecimal` in **major** units, so `1000000` denotes
MNT 1,000,000.00 = **100000000 minor units** (MNT, ISO 4217 numeric 496, minor unit 2; MNT
`decimal_places = 2` confirmed in `m_organisation_currency`). Recorded explicitly because the wire token
and the minor-unit value are different numbers and promotion must not conflate them.

## 3. Arm 2 — the measurement, the decision, and why I did not decline

### Measured (pasted rows in `ARM2-DECISION.md`)

- **`m_office` has exactly ONE row** — id 1, Head Office, hierarchy `.`.
- **`acc_gl_closure` was EMPTY (0 rows)** — the registry's claim confirmed against the live database.
- **60 journal entries, all office 1, `entry_date` from `2026-02-01` to `2026-08-01`**, 26 manual, across
  8 distinct dates.
- 7 loans, all office 1, all dated `2026-02-01`. Loan products 1-21 are `accounting_type = 1` (NONE, post
  nothing); 22+ are cash/accrual.
- Pre-mutation counters: `je 60 / max 64 / seq 64`, `closure_seq last_value 1 is_called f`,
  `m_portfolio_command_source 347 / max 347`.

### The brief's premise was wrong on the load-bearing point

The brief said a closure is *"hard-to-reverse"* and refuses entries at or before D **"forever"**. Against
source, on this codebase, **it is reversible**:

- `deleteGLClosure` is a **HARD DELETE** — `glClosureRepository.delete(glClosure)` [`:135`], and
  `GLClosure` has an `is_deleted` column but **no `@SQLDelete`** annotation, so the row really goes.
- The delete is reachable publicly: `@DELETE @Path("{glClosureId}")` on `/v1/glclosures`.
- It is permitted for the closure I would create: it throws only if a *later* closure exists, and
  `acc_gl_closure` was empty.

**I flagged this rather than quietly relying on it**, because my whole justification rests on a fact the
brief got wrong — if a reviewer disputes the `delete()` reading, arm 2 should be re-judged.

Confirmed afterwards **by observation**: 0 rows in PostgreSQL and `GET /glclosures` → `[]`.

### "Use a dedicated office" would have been WORSE, not safer

Only one office exists, so a dedicated office would have to be **created** — and **Fineract offices cannot
be deleted** (`OfficesApiResource` has GET/POST/PUT, **no `@DELETE`**; `m_office` is FK-referenced by
clients, loans and journal entries). That trades a *reversible* mutation for a permanent one, and
permanently changes the office population every future hierarchy capture observes. **Cost of that route,
as the brief asked: one permanent `m_office` row, permanently altered `GET /offices` output, plus its own
audit row — and still not deletable.** Rejected.

### Decision: SAFE → proceed, closure at office 1, `2026-01-31`, deleted immediately

The date was chosen so the plan is **safe even if the delete had failed**: `2026-01-31` is strictly before
the earliest existing journal entry (`2026-02-01`), and the refusal boundary is inclusive
(`transactionDate <= closingDate`), so it forbids **nothing that exists** — not one of the 60 entries, not
one of the 7 loans. It also cannot poison anything `tierA-a2/CAPTURE-PLAN.md` §5 still intends: every
remaining item there is a GL-mapping/product capture that posts no journal entry and is not date-sensitive
(the one ledger-touching item, savings, is behind the deposit-activation `user` gate and out of slice).
No competing fire: T273 and T285 are both on `conformance.sh`/its review, not oracle captures; T275 is merged.

### Result

| probe | transactionDate | status |
|---|---|---|
| `A2-01-preclosure-on-date` | `2026-01-31` (**ON** the closing date) | **403** `error.msg.glJournalEntry.invalid.accounting.closed` |
| `A2-02-preclosure-before` | `2026-01-15` | **403**, byte-identical body |

**THE BOUNDARY IS INCLUSIVE AND THE MESSAGE IS MISLEADING.** An entry dated *on* the closing date is
refused, though the message says "cannot be made **prior to**". Code is
`!DateUtils.isBefore(closingDate, transactionDate)` → refuses `transactionDate <= closingDate`.
**A port written from the English message gets a strict `<` and fails open on exactly the closing date —
the day a period-end adjustment is most likely to carry.** This is the most valuable thing arm 2 produced.

## 5. A defect this rig introduced, caught before it landed

My first `cap.sh` wrote a **zero-byte `out/NAME.req`** for bodyless GETs. The repo-wide capture guard
`.softhouse/capture/lib/check_wire_float_roundtrip.py` derives its population as *"`*.json` under a `req/`
dir, plus `*.req`"* and `json.loads()` each one — so three empty GET artefacts **REFUSED the whole
capture-tree guard, for every rig in the repository**, with
`JSONDecodeError: Expecting value: line 1 column 1 (char 0)`.

Fixed at the root: a bodyless request now gets **no `.req` artefact at all** (it is not an empty request
body; there is no request body). Guard re-run green: **exit 0, 440 documents / 10074 numeric tokens / 425
request bodies / 8 capture rigs**, with this rig included in the graded population.

I found this only because I ran the guard before committing. **Worth generalising: a new capture rig
should run `check_wire_float_roundtrip.py <repo-root>` before its first commit**, since it grades the
whole tree and one malformed artefact takes every rig down with it.

## 6. What I could NOT verify — read this before promoting anything

- **The ACCEPTING side of both boundaries is UNOBSERVED, deliberately.** An entry dated `2026-08-23`
  (business date) or `2026-02-01` (closing date + 1) should be accepted — but accepting means **writing a
  journal entry into the reference oracle**, which would have cost the zero-side-effect property that made
  these captures cheap. So: the future-date boundary is proven *strict from above* only, and the closure
  boundary is proven *inclusive* but not proven *one day wide*. **A promotion task that wants the accept
  side must budget a real ledger write and say so.**
- **`A1-02` is CLOCK-RELATIVE.** `2026-08-24` stops being a future date on 2026-08-24. It is committed as a
  dated observation of the boundary, **not** a re-runnable assertion. `A1-01` (`2026-12-31`) re-runs as a
  refusal until 2027. **Neither should be promoted into a vector without a date strategy — this is the
  single most important thing for the promotion task to notice.**
- **Everything about the business date is contingent on `enable-business-date = f`.** If a later fire
  enables it, the boundary moves to whatever `m_business_date` says.
- **Only the MANUAL journal-entry path was exercised.** The same `checkForBranchClosures` test is called
  from ~14 automatic accounting sites (cash/accrual loan, savings, shares, client transactions,
  working-capital) plus the reversal path at `:392`. That a back-dated disbursement on an
  accounting-enabled product is refused under a closure is **predicted, not observed.**
- **Multi-office behaviour is UNTESTABLE on this tenant** — one office, and creating another is
  irreversible. The per-office scoping (no hierarchy walk) is a source reading, not an observation.
- **No opening-balance behaviour was captured**, because the cited lines implement none. See §1.
- **I did not run the full `conformance.sh`.** It is contended this fire by T273/T285 and I was barred from
  touching it. I ran the one repo-wide guard that actually reads `.softhouse/capture` — the wire-float

### The T58 verification — T198 is right, T196's wording is wrong

I did not assume either. `sed -n '705,712p'` of the pre-fix file:

```
        path = os.path.join(VECTORS, fname)
        if os.path.exists(path):
            raise SystemExit("refusing to overwrite an existing vector: %s" % path)
        with open(path, "w", encoding="utf-8") as fh:
```

The refusal is unconditional, inside the write loop, immediately before the open, with no
`else`, no flag, no argv and no environment override — and the script parses no argv at all.
Then I **drove it**: seeded with sentinels at three of its own target names, the pre-fix
bytes **destroyed 0 of 3** and exited 1 at the refusal, while T74/T61/T64 destroyed 13 of 13
in the same run. T58 was never one of the thirteen.

**But T58 was not clean either, and this is new.** Three gaps the existence check does not
cover, each measured:

1. **No authorisation.** The check is *existence*-keyed, not *authorisation*-keyed, so T58
   freely **creates** new files in whatever store it is pointed at. Observed in the RED
   transcript: it wrote `P-LAT-Q0a-…json` and `P-LAT-MID-…json` — two vectors I had *not*
   seeded — into the scratch store before refusing on the third. Adding a vector to the
   store changes what parity *means* just as removing one does. Against the live store
   today all 16 of its targets exist, so it refuses immediately; the exposure is conditional
   on any one of them ever being deleted or renamed.
2. **Not atomic.** `with open(path, "w")` on a *create* still leaves a partial vector if the
   run is interrupted between the first byte and the flush.
3. **`os.path.exists` follows symlinks.** A dangling symlink at the target reports `False`,
   and the write would then be created *through* it, outside the store.

So T58 is hardened too — with its original refusal left in place verbatim, since that is the
historical guard and what the measurement exercised.

## The guard — the established idiom, and where the domain forced a change

`.softhouse/handoff/t203_store_guard.py`, imported by all six. This is **T178's shape**
(`.softhouse/reviews/t47-probe/t178_guard.py`, itself T167's), the guard T187 put behind 25
`t41-probe` rewriters, with T178's exit-code meanings and T178's argv-only authorisation
rationale. Callers use the same import idiom as `t41-probe/edit.py:80-92`: own directory
inserted at the **front** of `sys.path`, `NAME` + `AUTHORISE_TOKEN` constants, import fails
CLOSED.

Two transpositions, stated up front so a reviewer can weigh them rather than hunt for them,
and both documented at the point of decision in the module:

1. **T178 pins one document by a BEFORE/AFTER sha256 pair.** A promote script writes *N new
   files* into a directory; there is no "before" content to pin. T178's content gate is
   therefore replaced by the store analogue: **the target must not exist**. That single rule
   is what kills truncation, and unlike T178's content gate it has **no authorised bypass at
   all** (arms G3 and G5).
2. **T178's target policy is "outside the repository working tree". That rule cannot
   transpose.** T82's prover — the established tool for these very scripts — repoints the
   output dir at a scratch directory *inside* the repo and calls `main()`. Refusing in-repo
   targets would break the tool that proves these scripts behave. The protected artefact
   here is the **live store** (`<repo>/.softhouse/vectors`), and that is what the policy
   names, exactly as T178 names DEC-1 and `contract.go`.
3. `_atomic_create` is `t178_guard._atomic_write`'s sequence in the same order — mkstemp in
   the store's own directory, `st_dev` compared, `fchmod`, `fsync`, `os.replace`, `fsync` the
   directory. The one changed line is *what gets stat'ed*: a create-only writer has no
   existing target to stat, so it stats the store directory. That is why it could not simply
   be imported, and the module says so.

One shared module rather than six inline copies, per P-27. `os.path.lexists`, not
`os.path.exists`, so a dangling symlink is caught (G7). No bare `assert` anywhere (G8).

## Findings

**F-1 (against T196 and T198, HIGH). The population is six, not four, and two live-store
truncators were left unguarded by both.** `T57-promote-emi-vectors.py` (2 live vectors) and
`T8-promote-vectors.py` (**11**, including the baseline `P-00-baseline-6x7pct.json`) are bare
truncations of the live store, identical in kind to T74/T61/T64. T196's backstep classifier
unhid four; these two were never in the candidate set at all. Fixed here.

**F-2 (against the T179 classifier, HIGH — this is why F-1 was invisible).** T57 and T8
compute `OUT = os.path.join(ROOT, ".softhouse", "vectors", "loanschedule")` where `ROOT` is
derived from `__file__` at runtime. The classifier resolves mutation targets from **module
constants**, so the target scored **UNKNOWN**, not TRUSTED — and `--enforce` does not trip on
UNKNOWN. T196-1 closed exactly this fail-open for targets arriving as *function parameters*;
the **runtime-computed constant** case is still open, and it hid two live-store truncators in
plain sight for the whole of T196's and T198's analysis. Confirmed by measurement: a whole-
directory sweep of `.softhouse/handoff` reported T57's write under "UNGUARDED MUTATIONS WHOSE
TARGET COULD NOT BE RESOLVED", not under trusted-target sites. **Recommended:** extend the
resolver to constant-fold `os.path.join` over `__file__`-derived roots. Out of my scope
(`.softhouse/reviews/t179-guard-classifier/`).

**F-3 (against T196, MEDIUM). "Each is a bare truncation" is wrong for T58** — T198's
correction stands, verified by extraction *and* by driving it (0 of 3 destroyed, exit 1).

**F-4 (against T198, MEDIUM). "Cannot truncate" is true but is not "is safe".** T58's
protection is existence-keyed, not authorisation-keyed: it still creates unauthorised new
vectors in the live store (observed creating 2), is not atomic on the create path, and its
`os.path.exists` follows symlinks. Closed here.

**F-5 (INFO). The live-bytes arm shows all six promoters reproduce their live vectors
byte-for-byte from the pinned captures** — 42 of the 43 parity vectors are reconstructible
from source. Useful for disaster recovery; explicitly *not* a reason to consider truncation
harmless.

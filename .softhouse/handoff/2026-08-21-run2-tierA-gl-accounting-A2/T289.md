## F-T289-10 — The inclusive boundary: re-derived from source AND confirmed against the capture. They agree.

**Source** [`JournalEntryWritePlatformServiceJpaRepositoryImpl.java:634-640`]:

```java
final GLClosure latestGLClosure = this.glClosureRepository.getLatestGLClosureByBranch(command.getOfficeId());
if (latestGLClosure != null) {
    if (!DateUtils.isBefore(latestGLClosure.getClosingDate(), transactionDate)) {
```

`DateUtils.isBefore(LocalDate first, LocalDate second)` at `DateUtils.java:296-298` is
`second != null && (first == null || first.isBefore(second))`. Substituting: refuse **iff NOT
(closingDate < transactionDate)**, i.e. refuse iff **`transactionDate <= closingDate`**. Inclusive.

**Capture** — the evidence, and it agrees: `a2-01` submitted `transactionDate = 2026-01-31` against a
closure whose `closingDate` was `2026-01-31` (`req/a2-00-create-closure.json`, and `A2-00`'s response
`{"officeId":1,"resourceId":1}` confirms it was created), and the oracle returned **403
`error.msg.glJournalEntry.invalid.accounting.closed`**. An entry dated **exactly on** the closing date was
refused. Source explains it; the capture proves it.

**The message is misleading and T287 is right to shout about it:** *"Journal entry cannot be made **prior
to** last account closing date"*. A port written from that English gets a strict `<` and **fails open on
exactly the closing date — the day a period-end adjustment is most likely to carry.**

**What the Go port must implement, stated so it cannot be got backwards:**

> Let `C` be the closing date of the latest `GLClosure` for **this office** and `T` the submitted
> transaction date. **REFUSE when `T` is NOT strictly after `C`** — that is, refuse when `T <= C`, and
> accept only when `T > C`. **`T == C` is REFUSED.** In Go: `if !closingDate.Before(txnDate) { refuse }`,
> equivalently `if !txnDate.After(closingDate) { refuse }`. **Never write `if txnDate.Before(closingDate)`**
> — that is the strict `<` the English message suggests, and it fails open on the boundary day.

Two further port notes, from source rather than capture:

- **`args[0]` is the CLOSING date here, but the TRANSACTION date for FUTURE_DATE** (`:637` passes
  `latestGLClosure.getClosingDate()`, `:631` passes `transactionDate`). The capture proves it: `a2-02`
  submitted `2026-01-15` and got back `2026-01-31`. Same slot, different meaning, 6 lines apart.
- The lookup `getLatestGLClosureByBranch` is `where closure.office.id = :officeId` with a `max(closingDate)`
  subquery on the same office — **no hierarchy walk**, and **no `is_deleted` filter**. The Go port must
  scope per office exactly, and must not invent a soft-delete predicate the oracle does not have.

**Companion rule for FUTURE_DATE, same care:** `DateUtils.isDateInTheFuture(d)` = `isAfterBusinessDate(d)`
= `isAfter(d, getBusinessLocalDate())`, which is **strict**. So **`T == businessDate` is ACCEPTED** and only
`T > businessDate` is refused. The two guards in this one method therefore have **opposite** boundary
inclusivity, and a port that copies the shape of one onto the other is wrong twice.

# Date strategy — the conclusion the promotion task must start from

**A refusal vector that stops refusing is worse than no vector. A refusal vector that stops refusing *by
writing to the oracle* is worse than that.** Both apply here.

The structural problem: `FUTURE_DATE` is defined **relative to the business date**, so **no fixed literal
date is a permanent refusal**. Three strategies exist; two are wrong.

**(a) Pin the oracle's business date** — enable `enable-business-date` and seed `m_business_date`. Makes
literal probes stable forever. **REJECT.** It is a tenant-wide mutation that changes the meaning of every
other capture already taken on `gerege` (T287 correctly notes everything about the business date is
contingent on `enable-business-date = f`). Mutating the shared oracle to make one vector convenient is the
exact failure this review exists to police, and it is a program-level decision, not a promotion-task one.

**(b) Recompute the probe date at run time** (`businessDate + 1`) and re-fire against the oracle each run.
**REJECT.** It makes the vector depend on wall-clock, and on this tenant the harness cannot even *read* the
business date — `GET /businessdate` returns `[]` and `GET /businessdate/BUSINESS_DATE` returns 404 (both
captured, `M-07`/`M-06`), so it would have to re-implement `DateUtils.getLocalDateOfTenant()` in the harness.
Non-deterministic, and it re-fires a write-capable request at the oracle on every run.

**(c) LIFT THE BUSINESS DATE INTO THE VECTOR AS AN INPUT, and never re-fire the probe at the oracle.
ADOPT THIS.** The promoted vector becomes:

> **inputs:** `businessDate = 2026-08-23`, `transactionDate = 2026-08-24`, office 1, MNT, the captured
> debits/credits
> **expected:** HTTP 403, `error.msg.glJournalEntry.invalid.future.date`, the captured body byte-for-byte
> **provenance:** observed 2026-08-23T00:11:57Z; business date DERIVED, not read
> (`enable-business-date = f`, `m_business_date` empty ⇒ `BusinessDateReadPlatformServiceImpl` seeds
> `BUSINESS_DATE` from `DateUtils.getLocalDateOfTenant()` = today in `Asia/Ulaanbaatar`)

This (i) never expires, (ii) never writes, (iii) needs no oracle mutation, (iv) is fully deterministic, and
(v) grades the Go port on the thing that is actually under test — the **predicate**, not the calendar. It
is legitimate because Fineract's own business date is injectable (`ThreadLocalContextUtil.getBusinessDate()`;
`enable-business-date` exists precisely to make it so), and the Go port must expose it the same way.

**The same treatment applies to arm 2**, with the closure standing in for the business date:
`latestClosingDate = 2026-01-31` becomes an **input**, not an ambient fact. That also removes the
`resourceId = 1` / `id = 2` drift from the vector entirely.

**Promotability verdict, stated plainly:**

- **`A1-01`, `A1-02`, `A2-01`, `A2-02` are NOT promotable as-is.** As literal-date vectors they are time
  bombs, and two of them are already armed.
- **All four ARE promotable under (c)** — the observations are sound and sufficient; what is missing is that
  **the business date and the closing date are recorded in PROSE (`ARM1-OBSERVATION.md`, `T287.md` §2,
  `ARM2-DECISION.md`) and not in any machine-readable input.** The `.req` bytes do not carry them. **Lifting
  them out of prose into the vector's `inputs` is the whole of the promotion work, and it is mandatory, not
  cosmetic.**
- **`A1-01` is not "safer" than `A1-02` for being further out.** Its expiry is 2027-01-01, which is inside
  this program's horizon. The far-future date buys time, not safety.

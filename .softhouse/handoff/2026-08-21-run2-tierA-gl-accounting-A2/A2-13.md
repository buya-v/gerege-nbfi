## What DEC-2 covers, and what it deliberately does not

**Covers.** The GL account MODEL (`Classification` 1..5, `Usage` DETAIL 1 / HEADER 2, parent
link, hierarchy generation and its display decoration, `disabled`,
`manual_journal_entries_allowed`, `gl_code`, `name`); `acc_product_mapping` **resolution** (the
four steps — financial-activity pre-emption, core row, payment-type override, miss); **financial
activity accounts**; the **enum encoding rules**; and the **two-kinds-of-refusal taxonomy**
(contract sentinels versus oracle-faithful answers).

**Deliberately does not cover** — each named in §7 of the ADR, because a contract is defined as
much by its edges as its interior:

- **Journal-entry writing, reversal, and the sign convention** — slice **A1**. DEC-2 resolves
  *which account*; A1 writes the leg. **A2 fixes no sign convention and I invented none.**
- **`m_trial_balance`** — slice **A3**, and not portable as a balance store: its
  `closing_balance` is written at insert from an *unsigned* `SUM(je.amount)`, a written stored
  balance and a wrong one.
- **The chart of accounts** — data, G-9.
- **`Idempotency-Key`** — the adapter's HTTP layer and A1. DEC-2's surface exposes no endpoint
  and moves no money, and the ADR says so rather than leaving a reader to assume coverage.
- **Deposit-taking activation** — a `user` gate.
- **Amending DEC-1 or `contract.go`** — not required and not done.

**Two structural decisions I took and recorded rather than asked about** (both agent-decidable
under CLAUDE.md's answering gates; both reversible before ratification):

1. **§1.1 — DEC-2 does not create or freeze a Go file, and could not.** DEC-1 was written against
   an existing `contract.go` and its ratification froze that file's doc comments. There is **no
   counterpart** for this context: `nexus/internal/apps/ledger` is the *implementation* (it ships
   `InMemoryMappingStore`, `Resolver`, `InMemoryFinancialActivityStore`), and freezing an
   implementation as a contract would freeze its storage choices. So ratifying DEC-2 would freeze
   **this document's normative text only**; a separate task authors
   `nexus/internal/apps/ledger/contract` and a *later revision* pins its digest. Alternative
   rejected: writing the Go surface into the ADR as a fenced block and calling it frozen — a
   shape nobody has compiled or reviewed is a proposal wearing a boundary's clothes.
2. **§4.10 — I declined to invent a fifth capability status.** Several `ledger` capabilities are
   ABSENT for a reason the four statuses do not name: *no fixture exists on the capture tenant*
   (no savings product, no share product, no WCL product, no charge, no reason row). `unfixtured`
   would be the obvious addition, but `capabilities.json`'s schema is shared with `loanschedule`
   and changing it is not DEC-2's to do. ABSENT already refuses, which is correct; the reason
   goes in the row's `evidence`.

## The graded domain, predicate by predicate

Eleven predicates, each checkable in code without asking the oracle (ADR §4.2):

```
G-01  Seam                     ∈ {ledger_rest_admin, ledger_rest_posting, ledger_db_readback}
G-02  ProductType              == LOAN                              (stored value 1)
G-03  AccountingRule           ∈ {CASH_BASED, ACCRUAL_PERIODIC}     (stored 2, 3)
G-04  Entry                    == LOAN_PRODUCT_ACCOUNT
G-05  SlotFamily               == CashLoanSlot / AccrualLoanSlot, keyed on AccountingRule
G-06  PaymentTypeID            != nil    whenever SlotCode == 1 (FUND_SOURCE)
G-07  Currency.Code == "MNT"  and  Currency.MinorUnitDigits == 2
G-08  every amount's wire text is EXACT at MinorUnitDigits
G-09  GLAccount.Classification ∈ {ASSET, LIABILITY, EQUITY, INCOME, EXPENSE}   (1..5)
G-10  GLAccount.Usage          ∈ {DETAIL, HEADER}                              (1, 2)
G-11  for a POSTING-TIME grading only:  SlotCode ∈ {1, 2, 6, 12}
```

The four I would defend hardest, because each refuses something a naive contract would admit:

- **G-06** — `PaymentTypeID == nil` on the fund-source slot is **refused**. The oracle issues the
  payment-type finder with a null argument and **no null guard** [VERIFIED by me:
  `:1199-1206` has no `paymentTypeId != null` conjunct, *unlike* the WCL path at `:1015` which
  does]. Whether Spring Data renders that as `IS NULL` (matching the core row) or as matching
  nothing is **not settled from the pinned checkout and no capture separates the two readings**.
  Refusing is the only defensible answer, and the discriminating capture is named in the ADR.
- **G-11** — posting-time grading is confined to **4 of the 23 cash placeholders**. I verified the
  slot set myself: `A2-150`'s six journal-entry rows touch only `LOAN_PORTFOLIO` (GL 4, and GL 1)
  and `FUND_SOURCE` (GL 16, GL 2); `A2-235` adds `LOSSES_WRITTEN_OFF`(6) and
  `INCOME_FROM_RECOVERY`(12). The other nineteen are resolvable from stored rows and **never
  posted**.
- **G-02 / G-03** — LOAN only, and only CASH_BASED / ACCRUAL_PERIODIC. The tenant has no savings,
  share or WCL product at all, so four of the oracle's seven resolvers are graded by nothing.
  `ACCRUAL_UPFRONT` is refused rather than assumed inert by symmetry with the savings side.
- **G-09 / G-10** — out-of-range is **`ErrInvalidRequest`**, which the precedence rule puts ahead
  of any graded-domain refusal. This is DEC-1's §3.1 lesson (the `NumberOfRepayments >= 1`
  correction) applied pre-emptively rather than after a review found it.

**Two gradings the ADR explicitly separates from G-11** so nobody conflates them: *resolution
reproduced against the product read-back* (products 22, 28, 46) grades the mapping and the read
shape but **not a posting**; and *refusal parity* grades codes, statuses and messages.

**No sampling argument is made, and none is needed.** Unlike DEC-1, this context's inputs are
enumerable — product ids, product types, placeholder codes, payment type ids, classifications,
usages. What is *not* enumerated is the chart, which G-9 puts outside the contract.

## Money representation and the conversion boundary

**Go side, absolutely: `int64` integer minor units at every boundary.** No float, no
decimal-float, no `big.Float`, in any field, column, fixture or intermediate. MNT = 496, minor
unit 2; display 0, store 2.

**Oracle-facing wire, measured by me, not assumed:**

- **REST emits a bare JSON number containing a decimal point, in MAJOR units, at scale 6.** My own
  regex over the raw bytes of `out/A2-235-je-after-recovery.json` returns eight `"amount":`
  occurrences, every one of the form `N.000000`, **unquoted**. So an unguarded `encoding/json`
  decode routes MNT through a `float64` — the defect one remove from money code, sitting in the
  grading rig. (Contrast the vector store, whose own rule is that no JSON number may contain
  `.`, `e` or `E`; the *capture* files are the oracle's bytes and do contain it.)
- **PostgreSQL stores `acc_gl_journal_entry.amount` as `numeric(19,6)`** [VERIFIED by me twice:
  `A2-150`'s `information_schema` projection reads precision 19 / scale 6, and
  `JournalEntry.java:91` declares `@Column(name = "amount", scale = 6, precision = 19)`].
- **The same-shaped `amount` field also carries NON-money** — seven `chargeOptions` percentages
  read `1.234500`. So a blanket "`amount` → minor units" rule is wrong on its face; conversion
  must be per **field semantics**, never per field **name**.

**The boundary DEC-2 states, and only that:** conversion from the oracle's major-unit decimal
**text** to `int64` minor units happens **at the adapter edge** — the HTTP/DB decode for a live
adapter, the capture-transcription step for a vector — and never deeper in. Read the literal
characters, never a decoded number; **refuse** any non-zero digit beyond the currency's minor
unit rather than truncating or rounding.

**T186 is NOT pre-empted.** The ADR carries the marker verbatim: `[UNVERIFIED — T186 is settling
the general rule]`, and states explicitly that DEC-2 rules on nothing beyond the boundary this
context needs — not the general placement rule for other contexts, not non-2-minor-unit
currencies, not whether a shared conversion utility is mandated — and that **if T186 contradicts
§4.3, T186 governs and §4.3 is the amendment.**

This is the section B-5 forces, and it is the honest half of the contract.

| id | invariant | gradeable by a captured `ledger` vector? |
|---|---|---|
| **I-1** | debits == credits per transaction | **YES, today.** I re-derived `A2-235` myself from A2-7's leg table: debits `120,000,000 + 20,000,000 + 100,000,000 + 5,000,000 = 245,000,000` minor units, credits identical — matching A2-8's stated total. | **I-2** | splits sum to whole | **YES.** `120,000,000 = 20,000,000 + 100,000,000` (disbursed principal vs repayment + write-off), re-derived by me from the same legs. |

| **I-4** | append-only | **NO — STRUCTURAL ONLY.** "No UPDATE ever happened" is not observable from a capture. Partial exception: a reversal is observable *as a row* — `JournalEntry.java:79` carries `@Column(name = "reversed", nullable = false)` [verified by me]. |

## How G-9 and G-10 constrain this contract

**G-9 (CLOSED, chart of accounts) — I treated it as binding and did not re-decide it.** DEC-2
specifies the GL account MODEL, `acc_product_mapping` resolution and the posting rules; the chart
itself is seed data outside the contract, and a vector *names* the chart it was taken against
rather than embedding one.

Two contract facts I added that G-9 did not reach, both verified by me from `A2-150`:

- **`hierarchy` is an OUTPUT to reproduce, never a resolution input** — it is display-only and
  decides no money, and a parent's classification need not match its child's (GL 21 `Liability
  Under Asset`, classification 2, sits under GL 1 `Assets`, classification 1).
- **A HEADER account is a valid posting target** — `A2-150` journal entry id 6 debits GL 1, whose
  `account_usage` is `2`. A port that refuses a HEADER target would diverge **on observed data**.

**G-10 (OPEN) — recorded as a hazard, NOT decided.** The ADR states it with A2-11's two
corrections intact, because both matter and point opposite ways: (i) *"the read-back cannot reveal
it"* is TRUE, but *"nothing at the contract boundary reveals it"* is FALSE — one call cannot,
**two calls can** (`GET /glaccounts/2` shows INCOME plainly); (ii) *"five products"* understates
it — **five products, six mapping rows**, because product 27 duplicates GL 16 and GL 2 in a single
payment-type slot [I verified the duplicate myself from `A2-150`: `(27,1,1,pt 1) → n=2, {16,2}`].
The ADR also records that the tenant's **financial-activity** mapping is in the same state:
activity 100 = ASSET_TRANSFER points at GL 2, now `classification_enum = 4`, while
`AccountingConstants.java:439` requires `GLAccountType.ASSET` [both verified by me].

**The four admissibility rules it imposes — rules, not a decision of the gate:**

- **A-1.** A vector whose fixture is products 22/23/24/27/28, or touches GL 2 or activity 100,
  must **declare** that dependency. **Undeclared = INADMISSIBLE** (default-deny).
- **A-2.** **Resolution gradings are immune** and remain admissible — resolution never reads
  classification, usage or `disabled` (B-2, and A2-9 re-derived it for all four resolvers).
- **A-3.** **Write-side type-check gradings are NOT reproducible from a clean chart** and must
  carry the fixture state: under a clean chart `A2-214` would be a 200, not a 403.
- **A-4.** The driver's recommendation **(c)** is recorded; under (c) product **46** is a clean
  write-side fixture (`fundSourceAccountId: 16`, an ASSET — verified by me from `A2-211` and from
  `A2-150`'s GL 16 row). **DEC-2 does not decide the gate.**

## The creation-set vs posting-set divergence (A2-7)

**DEC-2 grades the POSTING set**, and specifies the creation set as a separate, weaker validation
predicate. That choice is the whole point of §4.7.

A2-7's measurement, restated as a normative rule: product 46 carries all nine `notNull()` slots
and no `ignoreIfNull()` ones, HTTP 200 — and `charge-off` returns **404 `… CHARGE OFF EXPENSE`**
and `goodwillCredit` **404 `… GOODWILL CREDIT`**. So
**{runtime} ⊄ {creation}**: a product Fineract will happily CREATE cannot complete every POSTING
path.

**The consequence a naive contract would miss, and DEC-2 states as a rule:** an implementation
must resolve **per posting path** and refuse **at posting time** exactly where the oracle does.
**A port that pre-validates the nine at product creation and then assumes every posting path
resolves is wrong, and would pass any vector taken only at creation time.**

**One thing I found that sharpens A2-7's own finding, and it constrains what those two captures
grade.** I read both loan enums in full myself: `CHARGE_OFF_EXPENSE` = **16** and
`GOODWILL_CREDIT` = **13**, and **both are present with identical names in both enums**
(`AccountingConstants.java:48`/`:51` cash, `:109`/`:112` accrual). So `A2-224` and `A2-225` grade
the *message text* exactly and **cannot discriminate which enum rendered it**. The rendering rule
(R-1: loan always renders through `AccrualAccountsForLoan` at `:1210`, WCL always through
`CashAccountsForLoan` at `:1026`) is therefore **source-derived, not confirmed at the boundary** —
and the ADR marks it `[UNVERIFIED at the boundary]` with the one-request capture that would close
it (code 22, 24 or 25 on a **cash** product).

The ADR also carries the update rule, which contradicts a plain reading of the update validator:
the create-mandatory set is re-imposed on update **iff the accounting rule changed**
(`ProductToGLAccountMappingWritePlatformServiceImpl.java:410-411`), observed as A2-240's 400
against A2-242's 200 control.

And it explicitly makes **no parity claim** about the five never-posted mandatory slots —
A2-7's probe set ran at a 0 % rate with no charges, no overpayment and no transfer, so that
absence is a statement about the probes, not the accounts.

## Gate needed? (yes/no + why)

**NO — no new gate is raised by this task, and no existing gate is crossed.**

- **DEC-1 and `contract.go` were NOT amended and no amendment is needed.** DEC-2 is a
  *sibling* contract for a different bounded context, and it contradicts no DEC-1 sentence.
  DEC-1's own §6.8 already anticipated this: *"GL and accounting (Tier A) — no risk. The ledger
  consumes a schedule; it does not change how one is generated."* Nothing in DEC-2 disturbs that.
- **G-10 is left OPEN and undecided**, exactly as instructed. DEC-2 records the hazard and derives
  four *admissibility* rules from it, which is a statement about which vectors may be promoted —
  not a choice between the gate's options. The driver's recommendation (c) is quoted as a
  recommendation, not adopted.
- **G-9 is treated as CLOSED and binding**, not re-litigated.
- **No `user`-gate class is touched**: no cutover, no regulatory sign-off, no licence fact, no
  real money, no live endpoint, no third-party binding.

**One thing the ratifier must decide, and I flag it rather than deciding it** (ADR §5). Ratifying
DEC-2 would freeze a contract whose graded domain is justified by observations that are **not yet
promoted vectors** — the `ledger` context has **zero** today. I enumerate three dispositions and
**recommend the third**: ratify, and require `A2-15` to promote at least one parity vector per
`in_graded_domain: true` capability before the context may be reported as graded at all.

**The argument for it is that it needs no new machinery, and I verified that in the grader's own
source rather than citing the README.** Three existing fatals already enforce it, all in

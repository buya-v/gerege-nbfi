## 2. The money-path finding is not lost — it is now an executable kill

Driver-verified at `:636`, and this is the whole reason A2-01 was worth promoting:

```java
// JournalEntryWritePlatformServiceJpaRepositoryImpl.java:634-639, pinned 426a23544
if (!DateUtils.isBefore(latestGLClosure.getClosingDate(), transactionDate)) {
    throw new JournalEntryInvalidException(GlJournalEntryInvalidReason.ACCOUNTING_CLOSED,
            latestGLClosure.getClosingDate(), null, null);
```

`DateUtils.isBefore(first, second)` is `first.isBefore(second)` for two non-null dates
[`DateUtils.java:296-298`], so the guard refuses **`transactionDate <= closingDate`** — INCLUSIVE.
The message it returns says **"prior to"**. A port written from the message gets a strict `<` and
**fails open on exactly the day a period-end adjustment carries**.

**The captured pair CAN distinguish the two readings, and the brief's question — which boundary does
each one pin — has a clean answer:**

- **A2-01 is ON the date** (`transactionDate == closingDate == 2026-01-31`). Inclusive refuses;
  exclusive **accepts**. **This is the only relation that separates them**, and it is what was
  captured.
- **A2-02 is BEFORE the date** (`2026-01-15 < 2026-01-31`). Refused under **both** readings. It
  pins **nothing** about the boundary.

So no additional capture is needed to settle inclusive-vs-exclusive. `LDG-REFUSE-04` is shaped so a
strict-`<` port **dies** to it: `ledger-wrong-closure-boundary-exclusive` is registered, executable
via `-ledger-impl`, and measured on the promoting run as
`KILLED — exit 1, ledger parity FAIL 0 + oracle-refusal FAIL 1` — it dies on that one vector and
agrees with the reference everywhere else.

`TestClosureBoundaryIsInclusive` walks **both sides** of the boundary (`D-1`, `D`, `D+1`) and asserts
the wrong port disagrees on **exactly one of the three** — the anti-vacuity control, since a
counterfactual that agreed everywhere would mean there was no defect to find.

## 3. T289's strategy was implemented, not refuted

T289 required `businessDate` and `latestClosingDate` to be lifted **out of prose and into the
vector's inputs**. Three new `Request` fields, in the shape T294 established for a *state*
precondition, now applied to the *dates* the rule was written for:

- `request.transaction_date` — `command.getTransactionDate()` at `:628`, the subject.
- `request.business_date` — what `isDateInTheFuture` → `isAfterBusinessDate` reads.
- `request.latest_closing_date` — what `getLatestGLClosureByBranch` returns; **empty encodes the
  oracle's own `latestGLClosure == null` branch at `:635`**, and on `LDG-REFUSE-05` that emptiness is
  **measured** (`M-04` and `M-05` are identical either side of arm 1: `closure_rows 0`,
  `je_rows 60`), not assumed.

**The reference implementation reads NO clock.** With no `business_date` it *skips* the rule rather
than defaulting to today —
`TestFutureDateGuardReadsTheBusinessDateAndNoClock/NO_CLOCK` drives an entry dated `2999-12-31` with
no business date and requires it to be **accepted**. A port that defaulted to `time.Now()` would
re-introduce the exact ambient dependence T289 rejected, and would go red there.

And the promoted vectors are date-stable by construction: the same relation shifted to `2030-02-28 →
2030-03-01` and `1999-12-31 → 2000-01-01` produces the same refusal.

**Eleven default-deny admissibility rules in `admit.go` stop the lifted fields becoming a hole**,
and all eleven are driven red in `daterefusals_test.go` behind a green anti-vacuity arm (P-22: a control
that cannot fail is worse than none). They refuse: a malformed date, a precondition with no subject,
a subject with no precondition, a closure claim with no closing date, a closure claim for a date
*after* the closing date, a closure claim for a *future-dated* entry (`:629` runs first, so the
oracle would have answered differently), a future claim for a non-future date (`isAfter` is strict),
and — the direction that would rot silently — **a parity vector whose dates trip a guard the oracle
checks before it ever gets to posting**.

## 4. The two NOT-PROMOTABLE verdicts, with the measurements

### A2-02 — the response bodies are BYTE-IDENTICAL

```
c12e977f1c633356e9679f9f2e691a35e1647bc4c19eefd2d6f55bb5c12805d2  A2-01-preclosure-on-date.json
c12e977f1c633356e9679f9f2e691a35e1647bc4c19eefd2d6f55bb5c12805d2  A2-02-preclosure-before.json
```

Two **different** requests, one **identical** response. It cannot diverge on any graded cell A2-01
does not already cover, so a vector from it would raise the corpus count by one and the kill count
by zero.

**But the byte-identity is itself the finding, and it is why A2-02 was captured.** Its
`errors[0].args[0].value` is `"2026-01-31"` — the **closing** date — while its own `transactionDate`
was `2026-01-15`. `:637` constructs `ACCOUNTING_CLOSED` with `latestGLClosure.getClosingDate()`;
`:631` constructs `FUTURE_DATE` with `transactionDate`. **The same wire field means different things
in the two refusals**, and A2-01 cannot show it because there the two dates coincide. T287 recorded
this in its handoff §1 item 4; the byte-identity is the direct proof of it.

`args` is not a graded cell — `Refusal` has three, and T294 **deliberately** declined to add a fourth
for `LDG-REFUSE-03` (its `args` is a 26-member tenant-state-dependent list that would go red on any
unrelated posting). Extending the shape would force a re-adjudication of an adjacent landed task's
reasoning, so it is **filed as backlog B-3, not taken unilaterally**. The finding itself is written
into both vectors' `_note`, into the port's source comments, and into the adjudication.

### A1-01 — no graded cell differs

A structural JSON diff of A1-01 against A1-02 reports **exactly one** difference:

```
DIFF .errors[0].args[0].value '2026-12-31' vs '2026-08-24'
```

Every graded cell — status, code, message — is identical. A1-01's relation (`businessDate + 130`) is
implied by A1-02's (`+1`) under any **monotone** date predicate, which the ported comparison is by
construction. A1-02 kills strictly more. The one thing A1-01 would catch — a non-monotone port
refusing exactly `+1` and accepting `+130` — is recorded so it does not have to be re-derived, and
promoting A1-01 later is a transcription from verified bytes needing **no oracle contact**.

value are different numbers and the vectors say so rather than conflating them. Nothing on a refusal

## 8. What is proven, and what is not

**PROVEN.** The oracle refuses a manual journal entry dated **on** the latest closing date, and
refuses one dated **one day after** the business date; both refusals are transcribed from committed
wire bytes with verified digests. A port reading the closure boundary as the error message describes
it is killed by `LDG-REFUSE-04` and by nothing else in the corpus. A port that never reads the
business date is killed by `LDG-REFUSE-05` and by nothing else. Both preconditions are inputs, so
neither vector can expire.

**NOT PROVEN, and stated rather than left to be assumed.**

1. **The acceptance side of both boundaries.** `[UNVERIFIED]` — backlog B-1/B-2, unfired by design.
2. **Strictness of the future comparison.** `>=` survives `LDG-REFUSE-05`. Source says `isAfter`;
   no wire observation separates them.
3. **The precedence of the two date guards against the later rules is SOURCE-DERIVED, NOT
   OBSERVED.** All four T287 probes are balanced and on manual-permitted DETAIL accounts, so no
   captured request violates a date rule *and* another rule. `:629`/`:636` sit inside
   `validateBusinessRulesForJournalEntries` before `checkDebitAndCreditAmounts` at `:650` and before
   `saveAllDebitOrCreditEntries` — unambiguous, but that is source, not wire. **Contrast
   `LDG-REFUSE-03`, whose ordering IS observed**, because T294 made its body unbalanced by one minor
   unit and the oracle chose. That option was not available here: these bodies were captured by
   T287 and cannot be re-shaped without re-firing them.
4. **`errors[0].args` is graded by nothing** — including the closing-date/transaction-date asymmetry
   that A2-02 measured. Backlog B-3.
5. **No balance is graded anywhere.** GATE G-12 remains open on
   `acc_gl_journal_entry.{office,organization}_running_balance`; neither vector's schema has a field
   for either column.

**And the standing one: PASS is not cutover.** Cutover from Fineract to Go remains a hard `user`
gate.

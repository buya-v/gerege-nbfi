## Changes Made

### Defect 1 — `on_signal` never reached the reconciler (attempt 1's work, re-verified by me)

**Located and confirmed on `main`'s bytes**, not taken on trust: `reconcile_tasks_json` was
*defined inside* `run_exit_guard()` (main:997) and *called* only from that function's body
(main:1032), in the script's normal tail. `on_signal()` (main:422) ran `stop_driver`,
`release_lock`, `exit $rc` — no reconcile. So SIGTERM/INT/HUP/QUIT terminated the fire
without repairing `tasks.json`.

The finding is **stronger than the brief stated**, and this is attempt 1's, verified by me
by reading the same bytes: because zsh creates a function body only when the definition is
*reached*, and `run_exit_guard` runs only after the first driver exits, on a first chain
iteration `reconcile_tasks_json` **did not exist as a name at all** when the traps were
armed. It was not merely uncalled; it was uncallable.

The fix moves the definition to top level and calls it from `on_signal` in the order
`stop_driver` → `reconcile (bounded)` → `commit` → `release_lock` → `exit`, with the lock
**still on disk** during the reconcile so `ready-tasks.py`'s ancestry check has something to
check. Supporting changes: `wait_bounded` (a bound in wall-clock seconds via
`zsh/datetime`, replacing two loops that counted `/bin/sleep` ticks and called the product
a timeout), `stop_driver` polling its grace rather than sleeping it flat, SIGKILL death
confirmed rather than assumed, and `foreign_live_session_in_repo` skipping the exact pids
this fire just stopped so the wrapper cannot refuse because of the corpse it just made.

### Defect 2 — the in-session authority. **Attempt 1's fix was armed and wrong in both directions; I replaced it.**

Attempt 1 split `caller_is_lock_holder()` into modes (`wrapper` / `in_session` / `refused`)
— that split is right and I kept it — and then made in-session demotion turn on
`task["fire"] != LOCK["fire"]`, arguing from the lock's exclusivity.

**The premise is sound. The data is not.** `fire` is stamped onto a task by the driver at
*first* dispatch and is **never refreshed on re-dispatch**. Measured, on this repo's real
state [VERIFIED, all from this checkout]:

| fact | evidence |
|---|---|
| fire `20260827-230001` took its lock at 23:00:08 on 2026-08-27 | commit `558ef32` |
| it marked six workers `in_progress` at 23:03:45 | commit `39d2156` |
| and a seventh (T305, `needs_retry → in_progress`) at 23:07:24 | commit `5d2164e` |
| every one still carries `"fire": "20260823-140001"` — a fire that ended four days earlier | `tasks.json` at `main` |
| every one still carries `"dispatched_at": "2026-08-23T03:57:24Z"` | same |

Driven against that state, in `in_session` mode, from a process genuinely inside a `claude`
with a genuinely-ancestral lock pid, attempt 1's resolver reports
`IN-SESSION authority: 6 demotable` and **would demote six live workers of the fire holding
the lock, T309 among them** [VERIFIED: `.softhouse/capture/t309-sigterm-reconcile-bypass/stale-fire-RED.txt`,
cell A of `ownership-matrix.txt`].

And it is wrong in the *other* direction too, on the very incident it was built for: the
eight corpses fire `20260823-140001` inherited from the SIGTERMed `080004` carry **no
`fire` field at all**, so attempt 1 withholds all eight — inert [VERIFIED: cell B; the eight
tasks at `5428c0a4` all have `fire: None`].

**The replacement is derived from doing the work, not maintained beside it.** The wrapper
commits `tasks.json` as it stood when it took the lock, under the subject
`softhouse: local fire lock (<fire id>)`. At that instant the fire has dispatched nothing —
so every task `in_progress` in *that* blob is an inherited claim, and every task
`in_progress` now but not then is this fire's own and is live. Two `git` calls for the whole
run (`git log -1 --fixed-strings --grep`, then `git show <sha>:.softhouse/tasks.json`), not
per task, so the wall-clock budget cannot be spent proportionally to the number of corpses.

This is the same property `.softhouse/LOCK`'s own comment already prefers for liveness —
push-recency over a `heartbeat` field, *"because push recency is DERIVED from doing the work
rather than maintained beside it, and so cannot silently fall behind the truth the way a
remembered field can"* [VERIFIED: `fire-program.sh`, the P-85/STEP 0 comment above the LOCK
heredoc]. **P-45 is "a guard that only works when someone remembers to run it enforces
nothing"** [VERIFIED: `.softhouse/patterns.md`, P-45]; `task["fire"]` is its field-shaped
twin — a guard that only works when someone remembers to *refresh* it — and the measurement
above is that nobody did.

`task["fire"]` is still **read**, but only as corroboration that is *reported* when it
disagrees. I also removed it from two other places attempt 1 had made it authoritative:

- **the demotion note**, which asserted `task["fire"]` as the identity of the killing fire —
  a false attribution written into the record the next fire reads back. It now says what
  each mode's evidence actually supports: `wrapper` mode names this fire as an
  **observation** (it stopped its own driver); `in_session` mode says "an EARLIER fire —
  established, not guessed … **WHICH earlier fire is NOT established**".
- **the `main()` report**, which advised readers that a task without `fire` is unclearable.
  It now prints `fire` *and* `dispatched_at` and says plainly that neither is authoritative.

## Fail-closed direction, stated per call site — they are opposite ON PURPOSE

The brief is right that these fail in opposite directions and that widening one predicate to
serve both is T292's shape. They are not widened; they are two predicates.

| call site | who calls it | fails towards | why that is right **there** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`wrapper` mode** — `on_signal` and the normal tail | `fire-program.sh`, after `stop_driver` has killed its own driver and `foreign_live_session_in_repo()` has *positively* established no other session is in the repo | **DEMOTING** every `in_progress` task | The caller has already established out of band that there is nothing live to destroy, so the only remaining cost is the `in_progress` lie — and P-85 is that lie. Refusing here would leave the exact state the whole task exists to repair. |
| **`in_session` mode** — a driver or worker | code running **while a fire is live**, so live siblings exist by construction | **REFUSING** — every unestablished leg returns `None`/`False` and nothing is demoted | The destructive error is demoting live work and it **cannot be undone**. The non-destructive error leaves a lie — and that lie has a second reader: the wrapper's own exit path, in `wrapper` mode, clears whatever is left on the way out. So this site leaves the lie standing and the *other* site is where it dies. |

The three-valued `foreign_live_session_in_repo()` (0 = foreign session found → refuse; 1 =
none, all decided → may reconcile; 2 = could not establish → refuse) is unchanged and is what
makes `wrapper` mode's demote-everything safe. Cell 3 of the SIGTERM matrix exists to prove
it still bites.

## Follow-ups

1. **`fire` and `dispatched_at` are stale on every re-dispatched task and nothing announces
   it.** This diff stops *depending* on them but does not *fix* them — the writer lives in
   the `softhouse-program` skill, outside this task's `files_hint`. Today 8 of 198 tasks
   carry `fire` at all, and every one of the 7 live ones carries a four-day-old value. Either
   re-stamp both on every dispatch, or delete the fields; a field that is present, wrong, and
   trusted is worse than an absent one. **This is the same shape as the defect it caused.**
2. **`softhouse/T309-…` vs `softhouse/t309-…` is one ref on a case-insensitive filesystem.**
   Branch names differing only in case will collide silently. `tasks.json` mixes cases across
   tasks (`softhouse/T297-review-t295` at `5428c0a4` vs `softhouse/t297-review-t295` now).
   Worth a normalisation pass and a dispatch-time guard. Note T299 is already assigned a
   namespace-collision task.
3. **A killed worker with real commits and a placeholder handoff is invisible to review.**
   Attempt 1 left nine commits of driven work under a handoff reading `(pending)` in every
   section. The reconciler demotes such a task to `needs_retry` with `WIP=commits` in the
   note, which is correct, but nothing tells a re-dispatched worker "there are nine commits
   on your branch — read them." I only found them because `git branch --list '*309*'` was on
   my way. Consider making the demotion note lead with the commit count and head sha.
4. **The signal path deliberately skips the worktree WIP sweep** (it cannot be fitted into
   single-digit seconds). Uncommitted worker WIP is therefore not rescued on a SIGTERM; it is
   swept by the *next* fire's `run_exit_guard`. That is a one-fire delay, not destruction,
   but it is an accepted cost worth re-examining if fires start being SIGTERMed often.
5. **None of this code is covered by `conformance.sh`.** `conformance.sh` greps for no
   `fire-program.sh` or `ready-tasks.py` behaviour, so every guard in this file is verified
   only by the harnesses in `.softhouse/capture/t309-sigterm-reconcile-bypass/`, which nothing
   runs automatically. That is P-45's shape one level out: the guards are now wired to the
   right path, but the *tests* for them are wired to nothing.

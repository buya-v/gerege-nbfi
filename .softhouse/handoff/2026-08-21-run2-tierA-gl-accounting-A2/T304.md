### The guard's own fail-open, committed rather than quietly fixed

The first version of `refuse-if-tracked.sh` compared the target against
`git rev-parse --show-toplevel` **lexically**. The scratch clone lived at `/tmp/t304-scratch`;
the instruments computed `$HERE` as `/tmp/…`; `git rev-parse` answered `/private/tmp/…`,
because `/tmp` is a symlink on macOS. The two strings differ, so the guard took its
"outside the repo ⇒ nothing tracked ⇒ pass through" branch and returned a **MEASURED ZERO**
for a directory holding 145 tracked files. All eight instruments then ran to completion.

That is a fail-OPEN inside the guard written to close fail-opens — **P-81's exact lesson**:
*"`T252`'s own linter caught three of its own instruments, including five `|| echo "(not
printed)"` arms in the instrument written to expose that class. … Writing the rule does not
immunise you against it; only the guard does."* Both sides are now resolved with `pwd -P`
before comparison, unresolvable paths **refuse** rather than pass, and arm **R1b** of
`50-guard-red-green.sh` is a permanent regression test that reaches the corpus through a
deliberately constructed symlink.

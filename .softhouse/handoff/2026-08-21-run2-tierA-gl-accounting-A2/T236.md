## The convention decision, ARGUED (a/b/c)

**Chosen: (a) — mint `cap10.sh`, add a `SUPERSEDES` pointer in `SUPERSEDED.txt`, leave
`cap9.sh` untouched as evidence.**

T216 left this named rather than deciding, correctly, because deciding the convention
was not what T216 was asked to do. It is what T236 is asked to do, so here is the
argument, including the case *for* (b) and why it loses.

**The case for (b) ("a signal list is not a value any observation depends on") is
correct about DATA and still loses.** I verified this directly, not by repeating T216's
reasoning: `cap9.sh` writes nothing to `out/`/`req/` until a completed exchange, and the
trap only fires on interruption, so **no byte of any of the 175 manifest entries in the
A2-3xx series could possibly depend on whether `QUIT` is in that signal list** — the
trap never ran during any of those captures (they completed). If the only constraint
were "does this change any committed byte," (b) would be safe and T216's caution would
have been more conservative than necessary.

**But that is not the only constraint, and I went and checked whether a second one
exists — it does.** See the next section: `MANIFEST.sha256` pins `cap9.sh`'s exact bytes,
**by design**, specifically to catch an undeclared change to a script that produced
evidence (this is `manifest.py`'s own stated purpose, added by A2-5 for exactly this
reason, quoted below). That pin does not care *why* a byte changed, or whether the
changed byte governs data or only a signal-handling edge case — it is a byte-identity
check over the whole rig, and `cap9.sh` is part of "the rig." Editing `cap9.sh` in place
would make `manifest.py verify` report `CHANGED cap9.sh` the moment anyone re-derived
the manifest from a snapshot of `cap9.sh` taken *before* the edit (e.g. from the A2-3xx
series' own capture-time git history) — i.e. it would break the very audit trail that
lets a future reader confirm "this byte-for-byte script is what produced this evidence."
**T216's own conservative call ("no exception is recorded anywhere in this rig for
'safe' edits") turns out to be backed by a real, checkable mechanism, not just an
unwritten custom.** (b) is off the table for a verified reason, not a deferred one.

**(c)**: I considered "patch the seven `run-*.sh` recipes to call `cap10.sh` and
retarget future captures" as an addendum, and rejected doing so *to the existing
recipes* — they are the actual record of what ran to produce the A2-3xx/A2-4xx series
(per `SUPERSEDED.txt`'s existing `run-220-a2-7-runtime.sh -> run-220-a2-7-runtime-v8.sh`
precedent: a recipe that already ran is a record, not a rerunnable derivation, and
"fixing" it to say something different from what it actually invoked would misrepresent
the evidence). Any *new* capture recipe that needs an `Idempotency-Key` should call
`cap10.sh` going forward — recorded as a follow-up, not acted on, since minting or
editing new `run-*.sh` recipes was not asked for and no new captures were requested by
this task.

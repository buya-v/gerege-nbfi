  [VERIFIED: baseline run, this worktree].

The pin is `TestExemptionCountIsPinnedCorpusWide`. It asserts the number by **two
independent routes** so a defect in one cannot move both: `Summary.InvariantsExempted`
(from the grading loop, graded population) and `InspectExemptions(vectors).Declared`
(from the store, loaded population). It also asserts they agree, and says why they
can legitimately diverge (an exempting vector that is refused or errors).

**Why a test and not a hard cap in the binary.** A cap inside `Run` would refuse
legitimate corpus growth at RUN time, in a fire that has nothing to do with
exemptions, and the fix would be to edit the cap under pressure. The kill counts
are pinned in the test suite for exactly this reason (`coverage_refusal_test.go`),
and the precedent is worth more than the marginal coverage: moving the number
should be a deliberate edit a reviewer sees, in the same commit as the vector that
moved it. What **does** gate the verdict is the grounding refusal (§2), which
lives in `Admit` and is exit 2.

## 2. The two dangerous shapes, refused

### The rule, phrased positively (P-35)

The harness does **not** grep an exemption's reason for weasel words, and it does
not look for a bad shape. It asserts the property `report.go` already claims for
every exemption, in that file's own words — *"admissible only for a shape where the
REFERENCE ORACLE ITSELF violates the invariant and the implementation reproduces
it"*:

> **Re-run the exempted invariant against the schedule the capture recorded — the
> oracle's own output, honouring that same vector's `unrecorded_fields` — and
> require it to come back VIOLATED there.**

One positive assertion refuses both shapes and **distinguishes them by name**:

| invariant's verdict on the recorded schedule | exemption status |
|---|---|
| `VIOLATED` | `GROUNDED` — admissible |
| `HOLD` | `DECORATION` — shape (1), refused |
| `N/A` **with** cells NOT ASSERTED | ~~`RESTS-ON-WITHDRAWN-CELLS` — shape (2), refused, cells named~~ |
| `N/A` with nothing withdrawn | `NOT-EVALUABLE` — refused |

**CORRECTION (T230).** The table above is the rule **as T222 shipped it**, and two
rows of it are wrong. As reworked:

| invariant's verdict on the recorded schedule | exemption status |
|---|---|
| `VIOLATED` (with or without unmade assertions) | `GROUNDED` — admissible |
| **anything, with ≥1 assertion NOT MADE because a cell it reads was withdrawn** | **`UNDETERMINED-ON-THE-RECORD` — ADMITTED AND REPORTED, never refused** |
| `HOLD` **with nothing withdrawn** | `DECORATION` — shape (1), refused |
| `N/A` with nothing withdrawn | `NOT-EVALUABLE` — refused |

The withdrawn-cells test now runs **before** the status test, which is the whole
of finding T225-F1: T222 branched on `res.Status` alone, so a **partial** hold
(the invariant held on every assertion it could make, and one it could not) landed
on the `HOLD` arm and was refused as a `DECORATION`. Measured, not reasoned:
`T116-G8-FAMB-N108` with one repayment row's `principal_minor` withdrawn and
`balance_roll_forward` exempted → `HOLD` with 1 unmade assertion → `DECORATION` →
**INADMISSIBLE, exit 2** [VERIFIED: T225's probe, re-run by T230 on the merged
bytes]. See T230's handoff for why refusing the undetermined case is wrong and why
`DECORATION` / `NOT-EVALUABLE` are still exit 2.

This is not a whole-file source grep and it is not kept green by its own docstring
(the T164/A2-11 defect): it **executes the invariant implementations themselves**,
via `runInvariant`, over a schedule built by the same `RecordedSchedule` the replay
implementation uses. `TestRecordedScheduleIsTheOneBuilder` pins that there is one
builder — *"47 schedule vectors agree, 1 of them carrying placeholder cells"* —
with a P-35 fixture bite refusing an all-zero agreement.

### REFUSE, not report — argued

**Decision: `Admit`, as a refusal.** The governing precedent is finding **T9-F1b**
(`admitDivergentCell`): a structural kill naming a cell the same vector withdrew
from grading is INADMISSIBLE, not a warning, *because the store may not record a
claim that is not evidence*. An exemption is a recorded claim of exactly that kind
— "the oracle violates this invariant here". A decoration is that claim **refuted
by the vector's own record**; a rests-on-withdrawn-cells exemption is that claim
with **no record behind it**. Both are decidable **offline, from the file alone**,
with no implementation and no run — which is the signature of an admissibility
question rather than a grading one.

The alternative (report only) was rejected on the same ground the task raises:
the exempted count is quoted as evidence about how much of the corpus is checked,
and a count that can be inflated by an exemption that silences nothing is not
evidence. A number a reader trusts, kept honest by prose, is the shape this
program has already found six times.

**CORRECTION (T230) — the argument holds for ONE of the two shapes, not both.**
T9-F1b refuses a **self-contradiction inside one file**: the same vector says
"this cell diverges" and "this cell was never observed". A **decoration** is that
shape — the claim refuted by the record — and it is still exit 2, on this
argument, unchanged. A **withdrawn-cells** exemption is **not** that shape: the
exemption's claim is about the **oracle's behaviour**, the withdrawal is about
what the **capture transcribed**, and both can be true at once. *"The record does
not say"* is not *"the record says no"*, and concluding otherwise is finding
T58-N2 one level up — the very ruling that a cell nobody observed must be declared
and **not asserted upon, in either direction**. T230 therefore keeps the refusal
for `DECORATION` and `NOT-EVALUABLE` and reports the undetermined case; the
count-inflation objection above is answered by counting it **apart from**
`GROUNDED` rather than by refusing the vector. The claim-needs-evidence half of
T9-F1b is properly closed by **F-4 below** (require a capture citation on an
exemption's reason), which applies to all four committed exemptions — whose
reasons are uncited prose today — not only to the ones a capture gap made
uncheckable.

### The DECORATION shape — refused, and it belongs in `Admit`

The task asked whether refusing the decoration is affordable, and where it belongs.
**Affordable: it is one `runInvariant` call per exemption, over a schedule already
in memory, 4 calls on the committed corpus.** It belongs in `Admit` for the T9-F1b
reason above, and because the decoration and the dangerous pairing are the SAME
assertion failing two different ways — splitting them across `Admit` and the report
would put two halves of one rule in two places that can drift.

It is also a judgement this program has already made by hand, once: **T116 dropped
a proposed third exemption (`balance_roll_forward` on the two family-B vectors)
precisely because that invariant holds unexempted.** Shape (1) is that judgement
made mechanical, and the RED fixture is literally the exemption T116 dropped.

`invariant violations` and must partition the same set of assertions. Both scopes
are stated in `grade.go`'s doc comments.

**F-2 — CORRECTED (T230) AND CLOSED.** The word **"invisible" was wrong**: a
partial withdrawal was **run-fatal** — `DECORATION`, INADMISSIBLE, exit 2 — one
withdrawn cell away on either committed family-B vector, and the trigger was

apart, named in the report with its withdrawn cells, admitted, and pinned at 0
corpus-wide in `TestExemptionCountIsPinnedCorpusWide`.

**F-3 — the grounding check asks about the ORACLE, not about the PORT.**
The admissibility sentence is *"the oracle violates it AND the implementation
reproduces it"*. T222 asserts the first conjunct, offline. The second is left to the
cell diff, which is sufficient today only because every cell the two committed
exemptions read is a graded cell — the very corpus-dependence T220 flagged.
Proposed: a grade-time check that an exempted invariant is ALSO violated by the
schedule the implementation returned, reported (not refused) when it is not, since
a port that *satisfies* an invariant the oracle violates is a parity diff the cell
diff should already be reporting.

**F-4 — an exemption's reason carries no citation.**
